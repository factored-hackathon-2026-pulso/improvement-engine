## Findings

### BLOCKER for publishing/using the affected real-data aggregates — privacy floor counts events/exposure, not distinct people

**Location:** `scripts/aggregate/bank_cells.py:170,235-303`; `docs/data/bank-cells-metrics.md:7-12`.

M7/M8/M9 add one denominator unit per digital event, campaign send, or transaction, and M10 adds handled seconds then emits whole hours. The k gate tests numerator/denominator counts in those units; the customer hash split only assigns the customer's events to a half and does not count distinct customers in each released cell. Therefore a high-activity single customer (or a handful of customers) can supply ten or more events/sends/transactions/hours and satisfy the documented `k=10` rule. The output then has no evidence that ten people contributed. This conflicts with the R3 rule “aggregates only with k>=10” if, as required for privacy-safe subject aggregates, k denotes distinct subjects.

**Failing-test sketch:** build a fully synthetic fixture where one `customer_id` contributes at least 10 M7 events in one action/channel/period cell (and separately 10 M8 sends and M9 transactions); `build()` must not emit those cells as k-safe. A companion 10-customer fixture should pass only when both positive and complement supports meet the distinct-subject floor. For M10, use one synthetic customer with >10 handled hours and ensure duration does not masquerade as 10 contributors.

**Required disposition:** before treating these exports as privacy-safe evidence, use subject-level support for the suppression gate, including complementary suppression, or explicitly amend the policy to a formally reviewed event-level privacy definition. The current metadata and count units do not establish subject-level k.

### NON-BLOCKER — M10 “effective n” is not an inferential sample size

**Location:** `scripts/aggregate/bank_cells.py:51,170,299-304`; `docs/data/bank-cells-metrics.md` M10 section.

The aggregator floors sums of resolved/unresolved seconds to whole hours and calls these counts a “conservative effective n.” Hours are exposure, not independent Bernoulli trials; flooring numerator and denominator separately can also change the rate. The documentation appropriately calls M10 a re-expression of M1 and dependent on M1, so the finding does not invalidate its use as a descriptive workload view. It does mean that a binomial uncertainty/significance result or k-anonymity claim cannot be inferred from the hour denominator.

**Failing-test sketch:** create two synthetic datasets with the same unresolved-hours share but different numbers of customers/contacts and different duration concentration. Assert M10 is labeled descriptive/dependent and never promoted as calibrated evidence solely because its hour denominator passes a numeric threshold.
